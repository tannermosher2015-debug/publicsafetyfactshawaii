# Design spec: Haʻikū Fire Station / East Maui coverage gap

Date: 2026-08-13
Site: publicsafetyfactshawaii.org
Status: awaiting Tanner's review

## Goal

An educational PSF article that supports closing the East Maui fire coverage gap, published
before the Council's Housing and Land Use Committee reconvenes on **Aug. 24, 2026, 9 a.m.**

## Posture (decided)

**Support the gap, not the parcel.** The article argues that East Maui's coverage shortfall is
real, documented by the county's own study, and unresolved after 17 years. It does **not** argue
that Bills 89, 90 and 91 should pass, and it does not adjudicate the land title claim, the
pending Office of Information Practices appeal, or the burial question.

Why this and not direct advocacy for the bills:

1. PSF's credibility rests on being records-based and not taking sides it cannot document.
   The title question turns on facts PSF cannot independently verify.
2. It would put PSF against kuleana descendants and a live OIP appeal.
3. The record does not require it. Across a 5h39m hearing, **no participant argued East Maui
   should have no fire coverage.** The opponent proposed an alternative site. A councilmember
   proposed an alternative acquisition model. That shared ground is stronger support than
   endorsing one parcel, and it survives Aug. 24 whichever way the committee votes.

## Working title

**"Two Hours From Pāʻia"**, with the gold italic accent on "Two Hours" per the site's headline
treatment. Deck: what a standard of cover measures, and why Haʻikū still has no fire station
after 17 years. Slug is frozen once created, so the title can be changed later without breaking
links; the slug should therefore be topic-based rather than headline-based.

## Scope

Four coverage gaps the department named (Haʻikū, Olowalu, Central Maui, Kīhei), with Haʻikū as
the one currently before the Council. Broader than one parcel, still anchored to the live hearing.

## Structure

Target 2,000 to 2,600 words. Sections scale to the evidence behind them.

1. **Lede: the two-hour hole.** A Pāʻia engine responding toward Keʻanae is two hours from the
   town it covers. Not a crew failure, a map failure.
2. **What "standard of cover" means.** The teaching core. NFPA 1710's 240-second travel time to
   90% of incidents, four-person minimum. Stated plainly as a **consensus standard, not law**,
   applicable to career departments. What the clock means for fire growth and for cardiac arrest.
3. **The four gaps.** The department's standard of cover study named Haʻikū, Olowalu, Central
   Maui and Kīhei. Call volume has **tripled since 2003**. 14 county stations, 10 on Maui island,
   none in Haʻikū. Apparatus lead times around four years.
4. **Haʻikū by the numbers.** Nearest stations 6 miles (Pāʻia) and 8 miles (Makawao). Census
   series, with the growth figure corrected on the record (see Corrections below).
5. **Seventeen years.** Timeline: 2007 purchase, 2010 AIS accepted by SHPD, 2011 EA at $11.2M
   with wind turbines and a helipad, 2012 Council places the site inside the Maui Island Plan's
   Rural Growth Boundary, July 2024 FONSI, April 2025 Ka Paʻakai analysis, Jan. 2026 Planning
   Commission recommendation, May 2026 transmittal, July 29 / Aug. 10 / Aug. 24 2026 committee.
   Note what was already cut in response to community comment: helipad, turbines, and the water
   corridor across other parcels.
6. **What is actually contested.** Fair and complete, stated as unresolved, not decided: the
   title claim, the OIP appeal over the Ka Paʻakai analysis and Sunshine Law, burials up the
   ridge and the un-trenched waterline route, the closed water meter list, Site D. Includes the
   documentable detail that **the bills' own legal description recites Grant 6553 to Florence
   Wood**, the instrument under challenge.
7. **What nobody disputes.** The pivot. Nobody argued against coverage. The opponent of this
   parcel proposed a different site; a councilmember proposed a different acquisition model.
   Both are cited here as **evidence of shared intent, not as endorsed alternatives** (see
   section 8 for why one of them does not substitute). Planning declined to opine on title.
   OED's director said he would not stand against native tenant rights.
8. **What closing the gap actually costs.** There are two routes and a null option, not three
   routes. Priced honestly:
   - **Pass the bills.** Fastest path to a station, and carries unresolved legal exposure the
     county has not answered.
   - **Site D.** New pre-design, new EA, new Maui Island Plan amendment, new DBA, new community
     plan amendment, new change in zoning, new AIS. Restart from square one, on state-owned land.
     The only candidate outside Kuiaha, and therefore the only one outside the title claim.
   - **Neither.** The bills are filed, the gap stays open, and nothing replaces it. This is a
     real outcome on Aug. 24 and the article says so.

   **The Molokaʻi model is discussed here but is not presented as a third route.** A councilmember
   raised it on the record (buy a predisturbed lot with an existing house, add an apparatus
   shelter, skip the EIS), so readers who watched the hearing will ask about it and the article
   has to address it. It does not substitute, for five reasons drawn from the record:
   1. It does not escape the title claim. Williams claims the entirety of East Kuiaha and said
      three of the four candidate sites sit inside it. A purchased house in Kuiaha is in the same
      dispute; a house outside Kuiaha is Site D with an acquisition attached.
   2. The enabling code amendment does not exist. It was described in the future tense.
   3. The Molokaʻi reference station is not built. Construction is due to be pau by year end.
   4. Scale mismatch against the actual program: 10,360 sq ft with crew quarters and training
      space, a 1,600 sq ft apparatus bay, a 40,000-gallon tank and septic. A house with an
      apparatus shelter is a lower service level, not the same station for less money.
   5. No site, cost or timeline for a Haʻikū version was put on the record.

   Handle this in a short passage, not a section head. State what was proposed, state what it
   does and does not solve, do not mock it. It was raised in good faith by a member who also
   said she would not support dispossessing kanaka.
9. **The other three gaps have not started.** Olowalu, Central Maui, Kīhei.
10. **How to participate.** Aug. 24, 9 a.m. **Oral testimony is closed** (closed July 29).
    Written testimony only. No ask, no side.
11. **Sources.** Every figure linked.

## Editorial rules applied

- **No em dashes anywhere.** Site-wide rule.
- **Attack the gap, not the officials.** Existing PSF stance.
- **Do not name the Fire Chief personally.** Williams stated on the record that the Chief's
  response to his Ka Paʻakai recommendations "shut them all down." This is attributed to
  **the department's written response to the Ka Paʻakai recommendations**, not to the Chief as
  a speaker. Assumption, overridable by Tanner.
- Quotes from the Chief supporting the coverage case are fine and are used; the restriction is
  on characterizing him as an actor in the dispute.
- Native Hawaiian claims are reported, attributed and left unresolved. PSF does not rule on them.
- **Independence disclosure line kept visible**, not buried: PSF is not affiliated with HFFA,
  IAFF or any employer. The same hearing contains a firefighter-spouse ethics question with a
  Board of Ethics ruling pending, which makes the disclosure load-bearing rather than boilerplate.

## Corrections carried in the article

The department's figure of 23% population growth "between 2000 and 2020" does not match the
decennial census. Haʻikū-Pauwela CDP: 1990 4,509; 2000 6,578; 2010 8,118; 2020 8,595. That is
+23.4% for 2000 to 2010 and **+30.7% for 2000 to 2020**. The article states the census series and
notes the discrepancy. The corrected figure is larger, so this strengthens rather than undercuts
the coverage case.

## Verification gates before publish

Each of these is a claim the article leans on, so each gets checked against a primary source, not
a search snippet. Nothing publishes on an unchecked number.

1. **Aug. 24 meeting notice re-pulled.** Every link, dial-in and testimony address currently in
   hand comes off the **Aug. 10** notice, which says details are subject to change. A stale
   dial-in in a participation section is worse than none.
2. **Census figures confirmed on census.gov directly.** The QuickFacts scrape returned empty;
   current figures come from the Census-sourced decennial table. Re-pull before publish.
3. **NFPA 1710 clause and edition** confirmed from an authoritative source, not a vendor blog.
4. **Station counts (14 county / 10 Maui)** confirmed from mauicounty.gov, not a wiki.
5. **"Call volume tripled since 2003"** confirmed against the April 2024 source, ideally the
   After-Action Report itself.
6. **Whether a fire station needs a change in zoning on any site.** The article's cost comparison
   assumes an alternative parcel would still need entitlements, which is why Bill 91 exists for
   this one. Confirm against the Maui County Code which districts permit a public safety facility
   and under what permit, rather than inferring it from this project's application. This is the
   load-bearing assumption behind "Site D restarts from square one" and behind the finding that
   buying a house does not skip the process.
7. **`npm run build` passes.** The build is the real check on this repo, not tsc.

## Explicitly out of scope

- Any conclusion about who holds title.
- Any position on the pending OIP appeal or the pending Board of Ethics ruling.
- Any call to testify for or against.
- Any endorsement of Bills 89, 90 or 91.
